# Study plan rules

## Topic priority within a course

| Topic type                         | Share of time |
|------------------------------------|---------------|
| Blind spot (any level)             | Highest       |
| Weak + high-yield                  | Highest       |
| Weak                               | High          |
| Developing                         | Medium        |
| Strong                             | Low (review)  |
| Mastered                           | Review only   |

Topics with several open mistakes move up one row.

## Splitting time between courses

When the student has several exams, give each course a weight every day:

    weight = (100 − readiness) × urgency

where **urgency** is 3 if the exam is within 7 days, 2 if within 14 days, and 1 otherwise. Split the day's study time in proportion to the weights, rounded to whole 30-minute sessions. Every course with an upcoming exam gets at least one session every 3 days, so nothing is forgotten.

After a course's exam date has passed, drop it from the plan and give its time to the others. Ask the student how the exam went and note it in the sessions log.

## Spaced review

After a topic is practised, schedule reviews at **+1 day, +3 days and +7 days**. A review is a short set of 3–5 questions. If the student does well on a review, the next gap can double; if they do badly, go back to +1 day.

## Phases (per course)

- **More than 14 days to the exam:** cover every topic at least once, weakest first; add reviews.
- **4–14 days:** mostly practice and review; one mock exam each week; one mistake review each week.
- **Last 3 days:** no new topics. Day −3: mistake review. Day −2: full mock exam. Day −1: light review of high-yield topics and fixed mistakes, and an early night.

If two exams are close together, finish the last-3-days routine for the first exam, but keep one short review session a day for the second.

## Session size

- One session = about 30 minutes or 10–15 questions.
- No more than 3 different topics in one session, and one course per session.
- Fit the number of sessions to the hours the student has, and leave one rest day a week if there are more than 10 days left.

## When the student falls behind

Rebuild the plan from today. Keep blind spots, high-yield and weak topics, and open mistakes. Drop or shorten mastered topics, and tell the student clearly what was cut and why.
