# Example ExamMind session

A shortened example of what studying with ExamMind looks like. The student is preparing for two exams.

---

**Student:** I have a Cell Biology exam on 20 December and Statistics on 23 December. Help me study with ExamMind. *(uploads lecture slides and two past papers)*

**ExamMind:** Great, two courses, 15 and 18 days away. A few quick questions:
1. What format is each exam (multiple choice, short answer, calculations…)? Do you know the length and total marks?
2. How many hours a day can you study in total?

**Student:** Bio is 2 hours, 100 marks, MCQ and short answer. Stats is 2 hours of calculations. I can do 3 hours a day.

**ExamMind:** Here are your topic lists from the slides. ★ = comes up often in the past papers.

**Cell Biology:** ★ Cell membrane transport · ★ Mitosis vs meiosis · Organelles · ★ Cellular respiration · Photosynthesis · DNA replication · Cell signalling

**Statistics:** ★ Probability rules · Normal distribution · ★ Hypothesis testing · Confidence intervals · ★ Linear regression · Chi-square tests

Anything to add or remove? If not, let's start the diagnostic: 2 quick questions per topic. Add how sure you are with each answer: **1 = guess, 2 = fairly sure, 3 = certain**.

*(…diagnostic questions…)*

**ExamMind:** **Q5. Mitosis vs meiosis.** At the end of meiosis I, are the two cells haploid or diploid?

**Student:** Diploid, because the chromatids haven't separated yet. Confidence 3

**ExamMind:** Not quite: they're **haploid**. I'll explain properly in practice. Because you were certain, I've flagged this as a **blind spot**. That's useful to find now, not on exam day.

*(…diagnostic finishes…)*

**ExamMind:** **Diagnostic results**

| Course | Readiness | Strong | Focus first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell Biology | 41% | Organelles, Photosynthesis | ⚠ Mitosis vs meiosis (blind spot), Cellular respiration |
| Statistics | 33% | Probability rules | Hypothesis testing, Linear regression |

Your plan for the next 3 days (I've saved it all in your **ExamMind** progress sheet):

| Day | Cell Biology | Statistics |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Mitosis vs meiosis · Respiration (1 h) | Hypothesis testing (2 h) |

Say **quiz me** whenever you're ready.

**ExamMind:** ✅ Correct, 4 chromosomes, so the cells are haploid (n = 4). Each chromosome still has two chromatids, which is why it's easy to think "diploid". What counts is the number of chromosomes, not chromatids.
